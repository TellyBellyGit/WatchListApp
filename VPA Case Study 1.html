The key lesson
A red candle is not automatically a “seller candle”. Read the candle in context: what happened immediately before it, how much volume traded, what price achieved, and what happened afterwards.

1. The market is an auction

At every moment, buyers and sellers are competing for execution. Some participants are passive, waiting with limit orders. Others are aggressive, willing to cross the spread and trade immediately.

5. Apply it to the blue-arrow candle

Now move from theory to the actual chart. We cannot reconstruct the exact Level 2 events from the finished candle alone, but we can build a logical auction hypothesis.

1
Strong expansion comes first.
The preceding candle makes a large upward move on strong volume. This is evidence that demand was capable of moving price substantially higher.
2
Buyers attempt continuation.
As the blue-arrow candle develops, buyers can still lift the Ask and price initially trades higher. This creates the upper wick.
3
Higher-price supply appears.
At higher prices, selling liquidity and/or profit-taking can oppose the aggressive buying. The important observation is that upward progress becomes difficult.
4
Buying fails to hold the highs.
The market gives back the upper part of the move. The candle closes below its open, producing a red body.
5
VPA asks: effort versus result.
There was meaningful activity, but the result was not sustained upward progress. That is a reason to investigate supply rather than blindly label the candle “bearish”.
6
What happens next matters.
The subsequent inability to break and hold above the candle’s high provides additional information. Later weakness can support the interpretation retrospectively, but the blue-arrow candle itself did not guarantee a next-day decline.

6. Effort vs Result — the VPA core

EFFORT

Volume

How much trading activity took place?

RESULT

Price movement

How much progress did that activity actually produce?

High effort + poor expected result = investigate.

If substantial volume accompanies an attempted advance but price cannot maintain the higher prices, ask what is opposing the advance. Supply absorption is one possible explanation.

7. What the candle does NOT prove

Not proof of a reversal

A single red candle does not prove the trend has reversed.

Not proof of hidden orders

The finished candle cannot tell you exactly which Level 2 orders existed or whether hidden liquidity was involved.

Not proof of seller dominance

High volume means lots of shares changed hands. It does not, by itself, tell you which side will win next.

8. Three beginner rules to remember

Rule 1

Limit orders provide displayed liquidity.
Market orders provide urgency.

Rule 2

Watch what liquidity does when it is attacked.
Absorption and consumption are more informative than a large number sitting on the ladder.

Rule 3

Never read a candle in isolation.
Read price, volume, location, preceding action and subsequent confirmation together.
