Strength Guide

Open a chart. Assess the evidence. Decide whether the stock is STRONG, WEAK or DEAD — then separately decide whether there is a sensible trade. Designed for news spikes, failed pumps and quiet grinders, at any time of the session.

The central question: Is the stock sustaining upward progress after the initial excitement, or did it merely spike and give the move back? Price shows what is happening; volume helps judge the quality of that movement. Neither guarantees what happens next.

1. Classify the stock's current condition

Judge the combined evidence. No single indicator decides the category.

Buyers maintaining control

STRONG

A sustained, orderly advance — not merely a burst of green candles.

  • Higher highs and higher lows are developing.
  • Pullbacks are controlled; price holds gains and buyers return.
  • Price holds above VWAP, or reclaims it and establishes support.
  • 9 EMA is above 21 EMA, ideally with both rising.
  • Breakouts show follow-through instead of immediate reversal.
  • Volume expands on meaningful advances and breakouts; pullbacks often have lighter volume.
Meaning: Worth watching for a sensible entry. Not an automatic buy.
Control is uncertain

WEAK

The stock may rally, but sustained upward progress is not established.

  • Price struggles to make higher highs; bounces form lower highs.
  • Gains are repeatedly surrendered.
  • Price crosses VWAP repeatedly rather than holding one side.
  • 9/21 EMAs are flat, tangled or frequently crossing.
  • Price rises on unusually thin volume or breakouts lack participation.
  • At resistance, price stalls while buying volume fails to expand.
Meaning: Unconfirmed or mixed. Wait for clearer evidence rather than forcing a trade.
Upward move failed / no usable momentum

DEAD

A practical trading label, not a claim the stock cannot move again.

  • A spike gives back most or all of its gains.
  • Higher-low structure breaks; lower highs and lower lows emerge.
  • Price loses VWAP and cannot reclaim it convincingly.
  • 9 EMA falls below 21 EMA and both turn down.
  • Heavy selling volume accompanies declines; bounces are weak or low-volume.
  • Attempts to recover are repeatedly sold into.
Meaning: Do not treat the original spike as evidence that another rally is due.

2. Read volume in context

Volume is participation — interpret it alongside the price movement, not in isolation.

Important nuance: Low volume is not automatically bearish. A low-volume pullback during an established uptrend can be healthy. A low-volume rise, however, is not automatically strong: it may simply mean there are few sellers. Compare similar one-minute bars and recent activity; the open naturally has more volume than midday. If available, use relative volume and dollar volume as context, not as magic thresholds.

4. Stock condition is not the same as a trade

A strong stock can still be a poor entry if it is extended or has little room to resistance.

ENTER SETUP

Structure + location + risk

Consider a trade only when there is a defined trigger, a clear invalidation level, and enough potential reward to justify the stop. For example, a controlled pullback that holds support, followed by renewed buying.

WAIT

Strong, but no entry yet

The trend may be healthy, but price is extended, volume confirmation is missing, or a pullback/retest has not formed. Do not chase simply because the chart looks impressive.

AVOID

Failed structure or poor execution

Price has lost key support, selling is expanding, recovery attempts fail, spread is too wide, or the next resistance leaves insufficient room. Do not assume a fallen spike must bounce.

Before every entry, write two things: “I am entering because…” and “This trade is wrong if…”. If you cannot state both clearly, the chart has not given you a complete trade plan.

5. Visual patterns to recognise

Schematic illustrations only — not actual market data or guarantees.

SUSTAINED ADVANCE

  • Higher highs and higher lows.
  • Pullbacks are controlled and gains persist.
  • Breakouts attract participation; pullbacks often quieten.
Read: Strong if volume and structure agree; still wait for an entry.

SPIKE THEN FAILURE

  • Initial surge is quickly surrendered.
  • Lower highs form; support/VWAP fails.
  • Heavy selling; rebounds lack participation.
Read: Weak or dead depending on whether breakdown and failed recovery are confirmed.

THIN-VOLUME GRINDER

Price rises steadily but volume stays unusually low.

  • Do not automatically reject it: a lack of sellers can allow a slow climb.
  • Do not automatically call it strong: thin participation can mean fragile progress.
  • Check higher lows, VWAP, spreads and whether buyers appear at new highs.
Read: WEAK / UNCONFIRMED until price holding and participation clarify the picture.

LOW-VOLUME PULLBACK

An established uptrend pulls back on quieter volume.

  • Price holds a higher low or reclaimed breakout level.
  • Volume fades during the pullback.
  • Buyers return as price turns up, ideally with expanding volume.
Read: Potentially constructive; low volume matters only in context.

6. Quick rules

Keep these in view while scanning charts.

  1. Progress: Is price advancing over a series of candles, or did it merely spike?
  2. Structure: Are higher lows forming, or are rallies producing lower highs?
  3. Holding power: Does price retain gains and hold VWAP / breakout levels?
  4. Participation: Does volume support advances, quieten on orderly pullbacks, or expand on declines?
  5. Trend: Do the 9/21 EMAs support the structure, or are they flat, tangled or turning down?
  6. Location: Is there room to the next resistance, or would an entry be chasing?
  7. Risk: Where is the trade invalidated, and is the potential reward worth the risk?
Golden rule: A price spike is an event. Strength is the ability to hold gains, build structure and attract or sustain participation after the event. Classify the stock first; assess the entry separately. If the evidence is mixed, do not force certainty.
Educational decision aid, not financial advice. These categories are qualitative, not validated forecasts. Always consider spread, liquidity, volatility, execution and risk. A chart can change category quickly; reassess as new bars form.
